# Report

The full write-up for the supervision is [`Paper7_Supo3.pdf`](../london-variety/outputs/supervision/Paper7_Supo3.pdf). A longer report with every robustness check is [`report.pdf`](../london-variety/outputs/report/report.pdf).

## Question

How much do young adults value living in dense places, how much of that value comes from a preference for variety in consumption, and did dense places regain their young residents once the COVID-19 closures ended?

## Findings

1. Young adults left dense places when their consumption value closed and returned when it reopened. In London a borough with one standard deviation higher amenity density lost 5.2 net young residents per 1,000 relative to older residents in the year to June 2020 (p = 0.002) and gained 8.1 in 2022. Across England and Wales, with access to food venues as the measure of density, the 2020 coefficient is -5.5 and the reversal +11.1 per 1,000 with teleworkability and garden access held fixed (both p < 0.001).
2. The residential value of density in Britain arrives as one bundle. Access to food venues correlates at about 0.9 with job and resident density across districts, and in each of four designs built to separate variety, the variation in lost variety moved with density itself or with a neighbourhood trend under way before 2020. The variety near British homes is largely food and drink, with restaurants, cafes, takeaways, pubs and bars making up about 98 per cent of the venues within reach.
3. Dense places regained their young residents, with full recovery outside London. In the test of Davis and Weinstein the coefficient for residents aged 25 to 34 across 7,262 small areas is -0.51 (95 per cent interval -1.11 to 0.10), so the registered verdict is inconclusive, and outside London it is -0.71 (-1.37 to -0.05), a reading of full recovery.

## Interpretation and limitations

The migration response is a measure of the value young adults place on density beyond living space and remote working, taken from behaviour within places and years. Within London the response is indistinguishable from the response to teleworkability and garden access, which correlate with amenity density at 0.82 and -0.73. The national access measure was chosen after a registered test with venues per resident gave a reversal of -1.5 (p = 0.191). Access is built from the food hygiene register as held in 2026 and used for 2019, with district counts that correlate at 0.93 to 0.96 with the 2019 Business Counts. The 2022 return lasted one year. Separating consumption variety from the rest of the bundle needs weekly variation in which hospitality closes while workplaces stay open, such as neighbourhood card spending or phone mobility data across the tiers of late 2020.
